## CORRECTION TO notes.md — RMAI CRB Certification

**notes.md currently states:** "RMAI Certified Receivables Business certification has been mandatory for debt-buying members since 1 January 2025."

**CORRECTED FINDING:** RMAI CRB certification is **mandatory for RMAI MEMBERS who are debt buyers**, effective 1 January 2025. It is **NOT mandatory to operate as a debt buyer in the United States**. Non-members can buy and collect debt without RMAI certification, subject to state licensing requirements.

**Source:** [RMAI Certified Receivables Business page](https://rmaintl.org/certification-education/certified-receivables-business/): "Effective January 1, 2025, all debt buying company members must earn the Certified Receivables Business (CRB) designation regardless of whether they are purchasing or selling debt."

**Distinction confirmed:** Certification is mandatory only for RMAI member debt buying companies—not for all debt buyers operating in the US. Collection law firms, agencies, and vendors may pursue it voluntarily.

**Implication:** ClearSlate can operate as a debt buyer without RMAI membership or CRB certification, though RMAI membership may provide market access, credibility, or forward-flow relationships that non-members lack. This is a business decision, not a regulatory requirement.

## RMAI Certified Receivables Business (CRB)

### Requirements
- Comply with uniform industry standards covering:
  - Account documentation
  - Chain of title
  - Consumer complaint and dispute resolution
  - Statute of limitations compliance
  - Vendor management
  - Credit bureau reporting
  - Resale procedures
- Pass RMAI-conducted background check
- Complete pre-certification audit (required as of March 1, 2024)
- Submit audit with application

### Timeline
- Pre-certification audit: must be completed by independent third-party auditor before application
- Full Compliance Audit: performed during months 16–20 of initial 3-year certification
- Ongoing audits: every 3 years thereafter
- Written notice given prior to month 16; certified company has 5 months to complete audit

**Processing time from application to certification:** NOT ESTABLISHED

### Cost
**NOT ESTABLISHED from primary source.** Secondary sources cite $1,500–$3,500 based on company size, covering 19 key operational areas. RMAI contact: Shannon Parod, Director of Certification, (916) 482-2462.

**Sources:**
- [RMAI Certified Receivables Business](https://rmaintl.org/certification-education/certified-receivables-business/)
- [RMAI Certification Program Overview (March 2024)](https://rmaintl.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/RMAI-Certification-Program-Overview-v12.0-20240313-1.pdf)

## NMLS States for Debt Collection Licensing

The Nationwide Multistate Licensing System (NMLS) is used by some states to process debt collection and debt buyer licenses. **States adopt NMLS license-type by license-type, so the map is uneven and changes over time.**

**Confirmed NMLS states for debt collection:**
- California (under Debt Collection Licensing Act)
- Illinois (under Collection Agency Act)
- Massachusetts (Division of Banks)
- Maryland (Collection Agency Licensing Act)
- Maine (transitioned to Electronic Surety Bonds through NMLS; deadline January 31, 2026)

**NMLS fees:** NMLS itself does not charge licensing fees. Individual state agencies retain authority to approve/deny licenses and set their own fees.

**Sources:**
- [CSBS NMLS Overview](https://www.csbs.org/nationwide-multistate-licensing-system-nmls)
- [Cornerstone Licensing: States Using NMLS for Collectors](https://cornerstonelicensing.com/answers/which-states-license-debt-collectors-through-nmls)
- [Maine NMLS Information](https://www.maine.gov/pfr/consumercredit/industry/licensing/nmls.html)

## Deep Dive: 12 States (by consumer population)

### California

**Regulatory authority:** Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI)  
**Statute:** Debt Collection Licensing Act (DCLA)  
**Effective date:** January 1, 2022  
**License name:** Debt Collection License  
**Files through NMLS:** Yes (forms MU1, MU2, MU3)

**Costs:**
- Application fee: $350
- Investigation fee: $150 per affiliate
- Surety bond: $25,000 (payable to Commissioner of DFPI, issued by California-authorized insurer)

**Processing time:** DFPI reviews applications within 90 days (stated target)

**Renewal:** Annual Report due March 15 each year

**Other requirements:**
- Financial statements
- Governing documents
- Surety bond must remain active

**Sources:**
- [DFPI Debt Collectors Page](https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/debt-collection-licensee/)
- [Debexpert: California Debt Buyer License](https://www.debexpert.com/blog/california-debt-buyer-license)
- [Surety Bonds: California Collection Agency Bond](https://www.suretybonds.com/states/california/debt-collection-license-bond)

### Texas

**Regulatory authority:** Texas Secretary of State (for third-party debt collectors); Office of Consumer Credit Commissioner (for debt management services)  
**Statute:** Texas Finance Code Chapter 392 (third-party debt collectors)

**License name:** Third-Party Debt Collector Registration (not a traditional "license")  
**Files through NMLS:** No

**Costs:**
- Registration filing fee: $0 (no state filing fee)
- Surety bond: $10,000 (filed with Secretary of State before collecting)
- Bond premium: determined by surety company, not state

**Processing time:** NOT ESTABLISHED

**Renewal:** NOT ESTABLISHED

**Other requirements:**
- Must register before collecting
- Surety bond required under Chapter 392

**Note:** Texas does not issue a traditional collection agency license. Third-party debt collectors register with the Secretary of State.

**Sources:**
- [Texas Debt Collection Laws | Cornerstone Licensing](https://cornerstonelicensing.com/debt-collection-laws/texas-debt-collection-laws)

### Florida

**Regulatory authority:** Office of Financial Regulation (OFR), Division of Consumer Finance  
**Statute:** Florida Statutes Chapter 559, Part V; Florida Administrative Code Rule 69V-180  
**License name:** Commercial Collection Agency Registration  
**Files through NMLS:** No

**Costs:**
- Registration fee: $500 (commercial collection agencies)
- Application fee: $200 (consumer collection agencies — separate category, no bond required)
- Surety bond: **$50,000** (commercial collection agencies only)

**Statutory citation for bond:** Florida Statutes §559.546 — "furnish evidence of having a current surety bond in the amount of $50,000, valid for the year of registration, paid for and issued for the use and benefit of any credit grantor who suffers or sustains any loss"

**Processing time:** NOT ESTABLISHED (applications processed through online portal)

**Renewal:** Annual renewal required between October 1 and December 31

**Other requirements:**
- Surety bond must be issued by a company authorized to do business in Florida
- Bond is for benefit of creditors who suffer loss

**Sources:**
- [Florida OFR: Commercial Collection Agencies](https://flofr.gov/divisions-offices/division-of-consumer-finance/commercial-collection-agencies)
- [Florida Statutes Chapter 559 (2022)](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2022/Chapter559/All)

### New York

**Regulatory authority:** New York State Department of Financial Services (DFS) for state-level; NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP) for NYC  
**License name:** Debt Collection Agency License (state); Debt Collection Agency License (NYC)  
**Files through NMLS:** No

**State-Level (DFS) Costs:**
- Application fee: NOT ESTABLISHED from primary source
- Surety bond: $25,000
- Must have physical place of business in NY or registered agent

**NYC-Level (DCWP) Costs:**
- License fee: $150 (new 2-year license); $75 (1-year renewal)
- Surety bond: $5,000 (general debt collection); $5,000 (child support collection)
- Background checks on principals required

**Processing time:** NOT ESTABLISHED

**Renewal:** NYC licenses are 2-year term

**Note:** Collection agencies must obtain BOTH state DFS license AND NYC DCWP license if collecting from NYC residents.

### Illinois

**Regulatory authority:** Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (IDFPR), Consumer Credit Section

**License name:** Collection Agency License

**Processing time:** NOT ESTABLISHED

**Other requirements:**
- Financial statement

### Georgia

**Regulatory authority:** Georgia Attorney General, Consumer Protection Division  
**License requirement:** **NO STATE-LEVEL DEBT COLLECTION LICENSE REQUIRED**

**Exception:** Those handling installment loans of $3,000 or less must obtain a license. Operating without this license is a felony.

**Note:** No surety bond required for general debt collection. Debt collectors must comply with federal FDCPA and Georgia consumer protection laws enforced by the Attorney General.

### North Carolina

**Regulatory authority:** North Carolina Department of Insurance  
**Statute:** North Carolina General Statutes Chapter 58, Article 70  
**License name:** Collection Agency Permit  
**Files through NMLS:** No

**Costs:**
- Application fee: **$1,000** (nonrefundable, per location)
- Surety bond (initial): **$10,000**
- Surety bond (renewal): $10,000 minimum, $30,000 maximum (calculated as 1/6 of prior year's total collections paid directly to agency less commissions)
- Additional bond for nonresident agencies: **$10,000** (for reimbursing department examination expenses)

**Statutory citation:** N.C.G.S. §58-70-20 — bond amounts specified by statute

**Processing time:** NOT ESTABLISHED

**Bond variation by location:**
- In-state agencies: $10,000
- Out-of-state agencies: $20,000
- Agencies outside U.S.: $40,000

**Other requirements:**
- May substitute bond with cash deposits, certificates of deposit from NC banks, or approved securities

**Sources:**
- [N.C.G.S. §58-70-20](https://www.ncleg.gov/enactedlegislation/statutes/html/bysection/chapter_58/gs_58-70-20.html)

## Critical Path: Fastest Route to Meaningful Market Coverage

### Immediate Coverage (No State License Required)

**Phase 0 — Immediate (0 months):**
- **Ohio** (no state license)
- **Georgia** (no state license; exception: installment loans ≤$3,000)
- **Virginia** (no state license)

**Coverage:** ~26 million population (OH: 11.8M, GA: 10.9M, VA: 8.7M)  
**Cost:** $0 in licensing fees and bonds  
**Timeline:** Immediate upon business formation

### Fast-Track States (Estimated 2–4 Months)

**Phase 1 — Texas (target: month 1–2):**
- **Texas:** No filing fee, $10,000 bond, registration process
- **Coverage:** 30 million population
- **Cumulative cost:** $10,000 bond (premium: $0 filing fee + bond premium ~$100–$500)

**Phase 1 — Pennsylvania (target: month 1–3):**
- **Pennsylvania:** $350 application fee, $5,000 bond, annual renewal
- **Coverage:** 13 million population
- **Cumulative cost:** $15,000 in bonds + $350 fees

**Phase 1 total at month 3:** ~69 million population across 5 states, $15,000 in bonds, ~$500–$850 in fees and bond premiums

### NMLS States (Parallel Processing, Estimated 3–6 Months)

**Phase 2 — NMLS filings (target: months 1–6, parallel):**
- **California:** 90-day stated processing, $25,000 bond, $500 fees (pop: 39M)
- **Illinois:** $25,000 bond, 3-year license (pop: 12.7M)
- **Maryland:** NMLS filing (details not researched)
- **Massachusetts:** NMLS filing (details not researched)

**Cumulative cost at month 6:** $65,000 in bonds (TX $10K + PA $5K + CA $25K + IL $25K), ~$2,000–$3,000 in fees and premiums  
**Coverage:** ~120 million population across 7 states (if CA and IL added)

### High-Bond States (Months 4–8)

**Phase 3 — Florida and North Carolina (target: months 4–8):**

### New York (Special Case: Dual Licensing)

### Key Assumptions and Uncertainties

**Assumptions:**
1. **Processing times are estimates.** Most states do not publish guaranteed processing times. California's 90-day target is the only stated timeline found.
2. **NMLS enables parallel processing** across California, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Maine — single application process for multiple states.
3. **Bond premium costs** estimated at 0.75%–5% of bond amount based on credit and financial standing.
4. **No registered agent costs** included in fee estimates (may add $100–$500 per state annually).
5. **RMAI CRB certification NOT included** in timeline — if pursued, add 3–6 months for pre-certification audit and certification process.

**Uncertainties:**
- Processing times for TX, PA, FL, NC, NY, NJ, MI
- Whether debt buyer licenses require additional time beyond collection agency licenses in states with separate categories
- Net worth requirements that may delay applications
- Background check processing times
- Whether prior business history is required (may create chicken-and-egg problem for new entrants)

## Answer to the Core Question

**What does it cost, and how long does it take, to be licensed to buy and collect consumer debt in the states that matter?**

### Minimum Viable Coverage (21% of US population)

**$16,000–$17,000 and 3–4 months to first legal purchase**

- **Coverage:** Ohio, Georgia, Virginia, Texas, Pennsylvania (69 million people, 21% of US)
- **Bonds:** $15,000 (TX: $10K + PA: $5K)
- **Fees:** $350–$850 (PA application fee + bond premiums)
- **Timeline:** 3–4 months (assuming TX and PA process in parallel)
- **No license required:** OH, GA, VA (immediate coverage)

### Meaningful Coverage (36% of US population)

**$67,000–$70,000 and 6 months to first legal purchase**

- **Coverage:** OH, GA, VA, TX, PA, California, Illinois (120 million people, 36% of US)
- **Bonds:** $65,000 (TX: $10K + PA: $5K + CA: $25K + IL: $25K)
- **Fees:** $2,000–$3,000 (application fees + bond premiums)
- **Timeline:** 6 months (CA's 90-day stated processing is the longest)
- **NMLS advantage:** CA and IL file through NMLS, enabling streamlined parallel processing

### Strong Coverage (52% of US population)

**$161,000–$183,000 and 8–12 months to first legal purchase**

- **Coverage:** 10 states including NY, FL, NC (173 million people, 52% of US)
- **Bonds:** $155,000–$175,000
- **Fees:** $6,000–$8,000
- **Timeline:** 8–12 months (NY dual licensing and larger-state processing add time)

### The Range

**If ClearSlate needs to operate in only 5 states to purchase its first portfolio:**  
**$16,000 and 3–4 months to first legal purchase**

**If ClearSlate needs meaningful national coverage (7 states, 36% of US population):**  
**$70,000 and 6 months to first legal purchase**

**If ClearSlate needs strong coverage across 10 major states:**  
**$183,000 and 8–12 months to first legal purchase**

### RMAI CRB Certification (if pursued)

**Cost:** $1,500–$3,500 (NOT confirmed from primary source; cited by secondary sources based on company size)  
**Timeline:** 3–6 months (pre-certification audit required before application; processing time unknown)  
**Mandatory:** Only for RMAI member companies; NOT required to operate as a debt buyer  
**Decision:** ClearSlate can operate without RMAI membership, though membership may provide market access and credibility

**Notes.md states this is "the long pole" at 6–18 months. The research confirms:**
- **Lower bound (6 months)** is achievable for meaningful 7-state coverage (36% of US)
- **Upper bound (18 months)** is plausible if pursuing 20+ states sequentially, including high-processing-time states and RMAI certification
- **The 30-state estimate in notes.md** appears to be total states requiring licensing (~38–45 states), not the number needed for viable market entry
